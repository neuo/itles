### 逐条诊断
| 原 | 对 | 类别 |
|----|----|------|
| my career basiclly **tie** me | **ties**（my career=单数第三人称）| 🔴 **Pattern 18 -s — 低压抽查又掉（6/2 make→makes，今 tie→ties，连续两次实锤）** |
| like sitting … and **immerse** | … and **immersing**（like 后并列都 -ing）| 🔴 平行结构（口语新触发，=写作 W2-18 同根）|
| At **dust** | At **dusk**（dusk=黄昏；dust=灰尘）| 🔴 近形词（Pattern 16，意义完全不同）|
| with barely **people** around | with barely **anyone** around / hardly anyone | 🔴 barely/hardly 配 anyone（否定极性词），不配光 people |
| how peaceful **the nature** is | how peaceful **nature** is（泛指概念不加 the）| 🟡 冠词（=写作 W2-19 同根）|
| **basiclly** | **basically** | 🟡 拼写 |

### ✅ Wins
- `Having said that` **形式修对**（-ing 保住）—— 6/2 的目标错当场清掉。
- `immerse myself in` **in 用对**（连续两 session 稳）。
- 🌟 跨 session 调出 `tie sb to the city`（6/2 P3-6 范文 chunk）。

### 📄 整篇修复版（clean，朗读建语感）

> The plan I'd like to talk about is a family trip we'd been looking forward to for ages. The idea was to take my wife and our son to Dali — we'd planned to cycle along the lake and just take it slow, unwind together for a few days.
>
> But about a week before we were due to leave, a fairly urgent project landed on my desk at work, and there was no way I could get away. So we had to rethink the whole thing. >
> Honestly, I was pretty disappointed at first — and to be fair, I still feel a bit of regret about it even now. The hardest part was seeing them off at the airport: standing there at the gate, with the two of them waving back at me, I really didn't want to let them go.

### 🔑 内化的 4 个动作（shadow 时盯）
1. **串句 + 破折号挂细节**：`take ... to Dali — we'd planned to cycle...`
2. **因果串成一句**：`a project landed on my desk, and there was no way I could get away`（6/2 突破的招延续）
3. **场景状语**（她常 miss）：`standing there at the gate, with the two of them waving back at me`（with+名词+状态 = 6.5→7 texture）
4. **`Having said that`** 收尾转折

## P3 配对（6 问）— Band 7 model，供 shadow（今日未 cold，应 suzy 要求生成）

**1｜Do people often change their plans?**
> Yeah, I'd say so — probably more than they'd like to. Life just throws things at you: something urgent comes up at work, or the weather doesn't cooperate, and suddenly the plan's out the window. I know I do it constantly. Having said that, the people who cope best are usually the ones who keep their plans flexible in the first place.

**2｜Would you tell others if you change your plan?**
> It really depends who it affects. If it's just my own plan, I'll often keep it to myself — there's no point bothering anyone. But the moment it involves other people, like my wife or a colleague, I'll let them know straight away, simply because they've usually built their own plans around mine.

**3｜Why do parents still make plans for their children nowadays?**
> Well, I think it mostly comes from a good place — parents just want to give their kids the best possible start. Because they've been through it all themselves, they feel they can steer their children away from mistakes. For instance, a lot of parents map out which schools or activities their child should go for, years in advance. Having said that, some take it too far and end up planning every little thing, which doesn't leave the kids much room to figure things out on their own.

**4｜How does technology help people make plans?**
> Honestly, it's made a huge difference. The biggest thing is that everything's in one place now — calendars that sync across your devices, apps that remind you, maps that tell you exactly how long the journey takes. Take a family trip: I can book the flights, the hotel and even the restaurants in one evening, all from my phone. So it's taken a lot of the guesswork out of planning.

**5｜What kind of plans do people often make?**
> I'd say it ranges from the tiny day-to-day stuff to the really big life decisions. On the everyday side, it's things like what to cook or when to hit the gym, that kind of thing. But then there are the major ones — where to buy a house, when to have kids, how to plan a career. So it's a whole spectrum, from "what's for dinner" all the way up to "where do I want to be in ten years".

**6｜Do people like the process of making plans more, or carrying them out?**
> That's an interesting one — I think it genuinely splits people. Some get a real kick out of the planning itself; for them, half the fun of a holiday is researching it and putting it together. Others couldn't care less about the prep — they just want to be there, living it. Personally, if I had to pick, I'd say actually pulling it off beats the planning every time.

> **P3 公式**：`起手表态(I'd say/it depends) → because/简因加点 → like/take…例子 → so I'd say 收尾`。每篇用 `Having said that` 或 `it depends` + 因果串，非散句。
