## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. Do you think parents should teach their children how to protect the environment?**
Absolutely. I reckon habits formed at home tend to stick for life, so if parents recycle and save water, kids just copy them naturally. For instance, a child who's taught to switch off lights will probably keep doing it as an adult. Plus, it's hard for schools alone to undo bad habits picked up at home.

**Q2. What laws about the environment are effective in your country?**
Honestly, I'd say the plastic-bag charge works best — once people have to pay, they suddenly remember to bring their own bags. Recycling rules in big cities help too, since sorting your rubbish becomes second nature. But to be fair, some laws look good on paper yet aren't enforced properly, so they don't really change behaviour.

**Q3. Which do you think people prefer, rewards or punishment, when it comes to government intervention in environmental protection?**
I'd say most people respond better to rewards, honestly. When you offer cash back for returning bottles or cheaper bills for using less power, it feels encouraging rather than threatening. Punishment can breed resentment, and folk look for loopholes. Having said that, a mix works best — rewards to motivate, with fines as a backup for serious offenders.

**Q4. Is it easy for children in cities to get close to the natural world?**
Not really, to be honest. City kids are surrounded by concrete, so unless there's a decent park nearby, they rarely see proper greenery day to day. Their parents are busy, and weekends often get eaten up by classes. Having said that, a quick trip to the countryside or a botanical garden can still give them a real taste of nature.

**Q5. What can people do to protect the natural world?**
Loads of small steps, really. People can cut down on single-use plastic, take public transport instead of driving, and recycle properly at home. Joining local clean-ups makes a difference too, since rubbish on beaches and trails really harms wildlife. To be fair, none of it feels heroic, but those everyday choices add up across a whole community.

**Q6. Is it important to teach students environmental protection at school?**
Definitely. I reckon schools shape how kids see the world, so weaving in environmental topics early plants the right values. For example, a simple class project on recycling can spark genuine curiosity about waste. Plus, children often nag their parents afterwards, so the message spreads home too. It's one of the cheapest ways to build lasting awareness.
