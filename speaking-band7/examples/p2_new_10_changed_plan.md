## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. Do people often change their plans?**
Honestly, yeah, all the time. Life's just unpredictable, so even a well-thought-out plan can fall apart because of weather, money or work clashes. For instance, loads of folk book a trip and then a deadline pops up at the office. I'd say the people who cope best are the ones who stay flexible and don't cling to every detail.

**Q2. Would you tell others if you change your plan?**
It really depends who's involved. If the plan affects other people, then definitely — it'd be rude to leave them hanging. But for something purely personal, like skipping the gym, I wouldn't bother telling a soul. To be fair, with group plans a quick message saves everyone a lot of confusion, so I'd always give people a heads-up.

**Q3. Why do you think parents still make plans for their children nowadays?**
Well, I reckon it's because kids simply can't see the bigger picture yet. Parents plan their schooling or hobbies since they want to give them a decent head start in life. For example, signing a child up for swimming early builds a skill that'd be tough to pick up later. Still, the best parents leave a bit of room for the kid's own choices.

**Q4. How does technology help people make plans?**
Oh, massively. Apps like shared calendars and maps take all the guesswork out of organising your day. For instance, you can split a trip with friends, sync everyone's schedule and book tickets in seconds. Plus, reminders mean you're far less likely to forget an appointment. Having said that, I think over-relying on it makes us a bit lazy about remembering things ourselves.

**Q5. What kind of plans do people often make?**
Quite a mix, really. Most people make short-term ones — like sorting out their weekend, a holiday or a dinner with mates. But there's also the big life stuff, such as saving for a house or mapping out a career. I'd say the daily plans keep us ticking over, while the long-term goals give us something to aim for.

**Q6. Do you think people like the process of making plans more, or the moment of carrying them out?**
Honestly, I think most people enjoy the planning bit more. There's something exciting about imagining how a trip or party might go, and the reality rarely lives up to that buzz. For instance, folk spend weeks dreaming about a holiday, then it flies by in a flash. Having said that, a few people are doers who only come alive once it actually kicks off.
