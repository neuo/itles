So, the occasion I'd like to talk about is a family trip to Kyoto a couple of years back, when I wasn't allowed to use my phone inside one of the old temple halls.

We'd been to Kyoto three times by then, but this particular hall was new to us. The moment we stepped in, a sign made it clear: no photos, no phones. It was out of respect for the place, and to protect some really old artwork on the walls.

Honestly, my first reaction was a bit of annoyance. My instinct is to snap a photo of everything, so being told to put my phone away felt weird. But after a few minutes, something shifted. Without a screen in front of me, I started to actually look — the faded paintings, the wooden beams, the stillness. Even my wife and Muye stayed quiet.

Looking back, I'm genuinely grateful for that rule. I mean, it forced me to be present and soak it in. So yeah, that's pretty much it.
