## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. How do young and old people use mobile phones differently?**
Well, I'd say there's a clear gap. Younger folk tend to live on their phones — gaming, scrolling social media, messaging non-stop. Older people lean towards the basics, like calls or the odd photo. For instance, my grandparents only really use theirs to ring family, whereas a teenager's glued to a screen all day, juggling five apps at once.

**Q2. What positive and negative impact do mobile phones have on friendship?**
Honestly, it cuts both ways. On the plus side, phones let you stay close to friends who live far off, with a quick text or video call keeping things alive. But they can also water down real connection — people sit together yet stare at their own screens. So it's a handy tool, though it shouldn't replace actually being there.

**Q3. Is it a waste of time to take pictures with mobile phones?**
Not at all, within reason. Snapping a photo lets you hold onto a moment you'd otherwise forget, and it's done in a second. The problem's when people obsess over it — spending the whole trip behind a lens instead of soaking it in. So I'd say a few quick shots are fine, but you shouldn't let the camera run the show.

**Q4. Do you think it is necessary to have laws on the use of mobile phones?**
Definitely, especially around safety. Take texting while driving — that's reckless, so banning it just makes sense and saves lives. I'd also back rules on phones in schools, since they're a huge distraction for kids. But beyond clear cases like those, I reckon it's down to common sense rather than the law dictating every little habit.

**Q5. What are examples of good and poor phone manners?**
Good manners would be putting your phone away during a meal and giving people your full attention. Poor manners? Texting mid-conversation, or taking a loud call on a quiet train. For instance, nothing's ruder than someone scrolling while you're pouring your heart out. So I'd say it really comes down to reading the room and respecting others.

**Q6. How does the internet benefit people?**
In loads of ways, honestly. It puts a world of information at your fingertips — you can learn pretty much anything for free off a quick search. It also keeps people connected across borders and makes daily life smoother, like banking or shopping from your sofa. So I'd say it's reshaped how we work, study and stay in touch.
