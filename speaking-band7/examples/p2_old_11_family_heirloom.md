The way I got it is actually quite touching. He helped raise me when I was little, in a small city, and before he passed away, he handed it down to me. I mean, it isn't worth much money, but to me it's priceless.

## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. What things do families keep for a long time?**
Well, I'd say it's mostly old photos and bits of jewellery, like rings or necklaces passed down from grandparents. Families also hang onto handwritten letters and sometimes furniture. Honestly, what they keep usually isn't worth much money — it's more about the memories tied to it, a little piece of family history they can't really replace.

**Q2. What's the difference between things valued by people in the past and today?**
To be fair, people in the past valued things that lasted — solid furniture, handmade tools, items they'd repair and keep for life. These days, though, we tend to prize convenience and the latest gadgets, which we toss out pretty fast. So I reckon the big shift is from durability towards novelty and constant upgrades.

**Q3. What kinds of things are kept in museums?**
Loads of different stuff, really — ancient pottery, old coins, paintings, fossils, even dinosaur bones. Basically anything rare or historically important that helps tell a story about the past. Some museums also keep everyday objects, like old kitchen tools, since they show how ordinary folk actually lived back then. It's quite a mix.

**Q4. What's the influence of technology on museums?**
It's been huge, honestly. A lot of museums now use touchscreens, virtual reality and interactive displays, so visitors can explore exhibits in a really hands-on way. Plus, plenty of them have gone digital, letting you tour the collection online from home. I reckon it makes history far more engaging, especially for younger crowds.

**Q5. What are the benefits of technology for learning history?**
Quite a few, I'd say. Technology brings the past to life — documentaries, 3D reconstructions and apps let you actually picture how people lived. It also makes learning more accessible, since you can dig into archives or watch lectures from anywhere. Having said that, I reckon nothing fully replaces handling a real artefact in person.

**Q6. Why do people visit museums?**
Well, mostly to learn something and satisfy their curiosity about history or art. Seeing the real objects up close feels way more powerful than reading about them in a book. Plus, it's a lovely way to spend an afternoon, especially with family or friends. For tourists, it's also a quick window into local culture.
