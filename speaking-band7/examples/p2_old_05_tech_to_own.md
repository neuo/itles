**Q1. What are the differences between the technology of the past and that of today?**
Well, I'd say the biggest gap is speed and ease. Older gadgets were bulky and slow, and you'd often wait ages for them to do anything. Today's tech is sleek, instant, and packed with features. Take cameras — film ones meant guesswork and waiting, but now you see your shot the second you take it.

**Q2. What technology do young people like to use?**
Honestly, young people gravitate towards anything social and visual. They're glued to short-video apps, streaming platforms, and gaming gear, since those feel fast and fun. I reckon it's mostly about staying connected and entertained. For instance, a teenager would pick a smartwatch or earbuds over an old desktop any day, because portability really matters to them.

**Q3. What are the differences between online and face-to-face communication?**
I'd say the main difference is depth. Face-to-face, you pick up tone, body language, and little pauses, so it feels warmer and more genuine. Online chats can be quicker and handier, but they're often flat and easy to misread. For example, a text joke might land badly when nobody hears your voice behind it.

**Q4. Do you think technology has changed the way people communicate?**
Absolutely, it's transformed everything. We're now reachable round the clock, and a quick message crosses the globe in seconds, which would've been unthinkable before. But I'd say it's a mixed bag — people text instead of calling, so conversations feel shorter and shallower. Honestly, we connect more often but maybe less deeply than we used to.

**Q5. What negative effects does technology have on people's relationships?**
Well, the obvious one is distraction. People scroll through their phones at dinner, so they're physically together but mentally elsewhere. That slowly chips away at real closeness. To be fair, it also breeds a bit of envy, since folk constantly compare their lives to those polished posts online, and that can quietly strain genuine friendships over time.

**Q6. What are the differences between making friends in real life and online?**
I'd say real-life friendships build slowly through shared moments, so they tend to feel sturdier and more grounded. Online ones form fast around common interests, which is brilliant for finding your tribe. But they can be a bit fragile, since you never fully know who's behind the screen. Both have their place, honestly.
