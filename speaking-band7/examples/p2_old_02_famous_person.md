Honestly, it's a real mixed bag. On the plus side, a famous kid gets amazing opportunities and money pretty early, which most children can only dream of. But the downside's huge — they lose any normal childhood, the press hounds them non-stop, and they grow up with zero privacy. I'd say the pressure often outweighs the perks.

**Q2. What can today's children do to become famous?**
These days I'd say social media's the obvious route. A kid can post videos, build a following, and go viral overnight if they're lucky. But beyond that, raw talent still matters — being brilliant at singing, sport or coding gets you noticed. Honestly, consistency's key too, since posting once won't cut it. You've got to keep showing up.

**Q3. What can children do with their fame?**
Well, the smart ones use it for good, I reckon. They can raise awareness for charities, speak up about causes that matter, and become role models for younger kids. Plus, fame opens doors — sponsorships, scholarships, that sort of opportunity. Having said that, some just chase attention and waste it, which is honestly a bit of a shame.

**Q4. Do people become famous because of their talent?**
Not always, to be fair. Talent definitely helps, but plenty of famous people aren't especially gifted — they're just good at marketing themselves or they got lucky with timing. Think of reality TV stars who're famous purely for being on screen. So I'd say it's a mix of talent, hard graft, connections, and honestly a fair bit of luck.

**Q5. Is it easy to become famous in your country?**
Easier than it used to be, I reckon. With platforms like Douyin and Bilibili, anyone with a phone can build an audience from scratch. But becoming properly famous — not just a flash in the pan — is still tough. The competition's brutal, so you need a real hook to stand out. Most people fade pretty fast, to be honest.

**Q6. Do you want to be a famous person?**
Honestly, not really. I like my privacy too much, and fame seems exhausting — being recognised everywhere, having your every move judged online. I'd rather be quietly successful in my own field than chased by cameras. Maybe a little recognition for doing something genuinely useful would be nice, but full-blown celebrity? No thanks, that's not for me.
