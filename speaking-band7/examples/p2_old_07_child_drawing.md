## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. What is the right age for a child to learn drawing?**
Honestly, I don't think there's a fixed age. Kids start scribbling as toddlers, and that's already drawing in a way. I'd say around three or four is when it gets meaningful, since they've got enough control over their hands. But it shouldn't be forced — they'll pick it up naturally once they're keen.

**Q2. Why do most children draw more often than adults do?**
Well, I reckon it's because kids have loads of free time and fewer worries. Drawing's also one of the few ways they can express themselves before they're fluent in words. Adults, on the other hand, get swamped with work and just lose the habit. For most grown-ups, it feels childish or pointless, sadly.

**Q3. Why do some people visit galleries or museums instead of viewing artworks online?**
I'd say it's about the experience. Seeing a painting in person, you catch the scale, the texture, the actual brushstrokes — a screen flattens all that. There's also the atmosphere of a quiet gallery that you just can't get at home. For real art lovers, viewing online feels a bit hollow, like a postcard.

**Q4. Do you think galleries and museums should be free of charge?**
Ideally, yes. Art and culture shouldn't only be for people who can afford a ticket, so free entry makes them open to everyone. That said, galleries do need funding to survive, so a small charge for special exhibitions seems fair. I'd keep the main collection free but let donations cover the rest.

**Q5. How do artworks inspire people?**
In loads of ways, really. A powerful painting or song can stir up emotions you didn't even know you had, and it makes you see the world a bit differently. Sometimes it pushes people to create something themselves. For me, a good piece of art lingers in your head long after you've seen it.

**Q6. What are the differences between reading a book and visiting a museum?**
They're pretty different, honestly. A book pulls you into one story over hours, while a museum lets you wander and take in loads of bits at your own pace. Reading's more private and imaginative, whereas a museum's visual and often social. But both feed your mind, just in their own ways.
