## P2 答案

So, the home I'd like to talk about is my grandparents' place, in the small town where I grew up. They raised me there, so it'll always feel like home.

It's a modest little house with a small garden out the back, where my grandfather grows tomatoes and chillies. Inside, it's a bit old-fashioned, but it's cosy and warm, and it always smells of home-cooked food.

The reason I love visiting is that everything slows right down. My grandfather putters around the garden, my grandmother cooks huge meals, and we just chat for hours. It's the kind of place where nobody's rushing anywhere, and honestly, it recharges me every time.

That said, I wouldn't want to live there full-time. The town's pretty quiet and far from my life in Chengdu, with few jobs around. The slow pace is lovely for a weekend, but day to day I'd miss the buzz of city life.

So yeah, that's the home I love to visit but couldn't settle in.

## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. Do Chinese people like to visit others' homes?**
Yes, very much so. I'd say visiting each other's homes is a big part of Chinese culture, especially around festivals like Spring Festival. It's seen as warm and personal, way friendlier than meeting in a restaurant. Honestly, inviting someone over for a home-cooked meal is one of the nicest ways to show you really value them.

**Q2. What do Chinese people do when they visit others?**
Well, the host usually goes all out cooking a big meal, and guests rarely show up empty-handed. People chat over tea or fruit, and there's often a lot of polite back-and-forth about who pays for what. To be fair, it's pretty relaxed once everyone settles in, with kids running around and grown-ups swapping news.

**Q3. What kind of place do people in your country like to live in?**
Most people in my country lean towards big cities, honestly. Younger folk especially want to live somewhere with good jobs, transport and nightlife, so places like Chengdu or Shanghai pull them in. That said, plenty of older people prefer a quieter spot back home, where life's cheaper and the pace suits them far better.

**Q4. What's the difference between homes in cities and those in the countryside?**
The biggest gap is space, I reckon. City homes are mostly cramped flats in tall towers, while country houses tend to be bigger, often with a yard or a bit of land. Plus, rural homes feel more laid-back and neighbourly, whereas city living's faster and a lot more private, since people barely know their neighbours.

**Q5. What kind of gifts do people usually bring when they visit others?**
It really depends, but fruit's a safe bet, and so are nice snacks or boxed tea. If you're visiting someone with kids, people often grab toys or sweets. For closer friends, I'd say a decent bottle of wine works well. The golden rule is never turning up with nothing, since that'd come across as a bit rude.

**Q6. How often do you visit your relatives or friends?**
Not as often as I'd like, sadly. I usually see my relatives during big holidays, maybe a few times a year, since most of them live quite far away. With friends it's easier, so we'll grab dinner every couple of weeks. Honestly, life gets so busy that visits slip down the list more than they should.
