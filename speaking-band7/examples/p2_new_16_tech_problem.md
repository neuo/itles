## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. What are the advantages and disadvantages of AI?**
Well, on the plus side, AI saves us loads of time — it can draft emails, crunch data, even spot patterns we'd miss. The downside, honestly, is that people start leaning on it too much and stop thinking for themselves. Plus there's the worry about jobs disappearing. So it's brilliant, but you've got to use it wisely.

**Q2. Do you think people today should learn about AI technology?**
Absolutely, I reckon it's pretty much unavoidable now. AI's creeping into every job, so if you don't get the basics, you'll fall behind quickly. I'm not saying everyone needs to code, but knowing what it can and can't do is really handy. For instance, even checking whether an answer's reliable is a skill worth having.

**Q3. Should children learn to use AI?**
I'd say yes, but with limits. Kids these days grow up surrounded by it, so teaching them to use it safely just makes sense. The tricky bit is they shouldn't rely on it for homework before they've learnt to think on their own. For example, using AI to explain a tough idea is fine; copying answers isn't.

**Q4. How can AI help in our lives?**
In loads of ways, really. It takes the boring chores off our plate — sorting emails, booking appointments, translating on the fly. It's also a quick way to learn, since you can ask it anything and get a clear answer. To be fair, the biggest win is how it frees up time for what actually matters to us.

**Q5. Do you think students are overly reliant on AI?**
Honestly, a fair few are. I've noticed some students just paste a question in and copy whatever pops out, without really digesting it. The danger is they never build the skill themselves, so they're stuck when the tool isn't around. That said, it's not all of them — plenty still use it as a helper, not a crutch.

**Q6. What can teachers do to stop students from relying too much on AI?**
Well, the simplest fix is to set tasks that AI can't easily handle — things like in-class discussions or handwritten work. Teachers could also ask students to explain their reasoning out loud, since you can't fake understanding. To be fair, banning it won't work, so the smarter move is teaching kids when leaning on it actually helps.
