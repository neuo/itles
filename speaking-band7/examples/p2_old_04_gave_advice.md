## P3 follow-ups（答案）

**Q1. Should people prepare before giving advice?**
Yeah, I really think they should. If you give advice off the top of your head, you might miss the bigger picture and end up steering someone wrong. A bit of thought beforehand shows you actually care. For instance, before telling a friend to quit their job, you'd want to understand why they're unhappy first.

**Q2. Is it good to ask advice from strangers online?**
Honestly, it's a mixed bag. Strangers online can be brilliant for niche stuff, like fixing a laptop, since someone's probably solved it before. But for personal decisions I'd be cautious, because they don't know your situation and anyone can pretend to be an expert. So I'd take it with a pinch of salt.

**Q3. What are the personalities of people whose job is to give advice to others?**
I'd say they tend to be good listeners and pretty patient. A counsellor or coach, for example, can't just push their own opinions; they have to stay calm and read people well. They're usually empathetic too, so folk feel safe opening up. Being a bit objective helps, since they aren't emotionally tangled up in it.

**Q4. What are the problems if you ask too many people for advice?**
The main problem is you end up overwhelmed. Everyone's got their own take, so you get loads of conflicting opinions and freeze up instead of deciding. To be fair, it can also chip away at your confidence, because you start doubting yourself. Sometimes two or three trusted views are plenty, and more just muddies the water.

**Q5. Why do some people think it is better to ask for advice from friends than from parents?**
Well, friends often feel more relatable, since they're roughly the same age and get what you're going through right now. Parents can be a bit out of touch with modern pressures, and their advice sometimes comes with judgement. With mates, you can be honest without worrying they'll lecture you or worry endlessly about you.

**Q6. When would old people ask young people for advice?**
Mostly when it comes to technology, I reckon. Older folk often ask the younger ones how to use a smartphone, set up video calls, or shop online. They'll also turn to youngsters for fresh perspectives on trends or career paths, since the world's changed so fast and young people are right in the thick of it.
