## P2 答案

So, the place I'd like to talk about is Kyoto, an old city in Japan that I've been to three times now with my family.

It's down in the western part of the country, not too far from Osaka, and it used to be Japan's capital, so it's absolutely packed with old temples and quiet gardens.

On our trips, we mostly took our son to see the cherry blossoms in spring, and one autumn we caught the maple leaves turning red, which was honestly stunning. We wandered around Kiyomizu Temple, took loads of photos, and spent a whole afternoon in the bamboo groves at Arashiyama. The food there was pretty amazing too.

The reason I'd recommend it is that it really slows you down. Plus, it's got this timeless feel you don't get in big modern cities. So all in all, Kyoto's one of those places I'll always remember, and I'd love to take my family back again sometime.

**Q1. Where do people in your country often go for holidays?**
Well, I'd say most people here head to the coast or the mountains when they get time off. Beach towns are hugely popular in summer because folk just want to relax and cool down. But there's also a growing crowd who prefer quieter spots in the countryside, since city life can get pretty exhausting and they crave a real break.

**Q2. What is the ideal length for a holiday?**
Honestly, I reckon around a week is the sweet spot. Anything shorter and you barely unwind before it's over, especially if there's a long flight involved. A week gives you enough time to properly explore a place without rushing. But for a big trip abroad, I'd push it to two weeks, since you've travelled so far anyway.

**Q3. How do people usually plan holidays?**
These days, most folk start online, browsing reviews and comparing prices on travel apps. I'd say that's become second nature for everyone. Some like to book everything in advance, from flights to hotels, while others just sort out the basics and figure out the rest once they arrive. It really depends on how spontaneous you are, I suppose.

**Q4. Is it important to plan a holiday ahead?**
To be fair, I think it really helps, especially for a trip overseas. Planning ahead means you lock in cheaper flights and avoid the stress of everything being booked out. That said, you don't want to over-schedule and leave no room to wander. So I'd plan the big stuff early, but keep the daily routine flexible.

**Q5. Why do many countries try to attract people to visit?**
Well, it's mostly about money, really. Tourists bring in a huge amount of income, which supports local businesses and creates loads of jobs for ordinary people. Plus, it's great for a country's image, since visitors go home and spread the word. So countries pour money into adverts and landmarks to keep the crowds coming back.

**Q6. How do people decide when to travel?**
I'd say it usually comes down to the weather and time off work. Most folk wait for public holidays or the summer break, simply because that's when they can actually get away. Cost matters too, since prices drop in the off-season. So a lot of people time their trips around cheaper flights and better deals.
