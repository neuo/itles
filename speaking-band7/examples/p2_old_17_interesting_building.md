**Q1. What types of buildings are popular in your country?**
Well, I'd say high-rise apartments are by far the most popular, mainly because cities here are so crowded that building upwards is the only sensible option. You also see loads of shiny shopping malls and mixed-use towers going up. Traditional courtyard houses still exist, but honestly they're getting rarer in big urban areas now.

**Q2. Is it worth spending a lot of money on the exterior appearance of a building?**
It depends, really. For landmarks meant to attract tourists, I reckon it's money well spent, since a striking exterior becomes the city's calling card. But for ordinary offices, splashing out on fancy facades feels wasteful when that cash could improve what's inside. So I'd say balance matters more than pure showing off.

**Q3. Is it more important for a building to look good on the outside or on the inside?**
Honestly, I lean towards the inside being more important, because that's where people actually spend their time. A gorgeous exterior fades after the first glance, but a well-designed, practical interior keeps mattering day after day. That said, a building really should look decent outside too, so first impressions aren't ruined.

**Q4. Why do people like to visit historical sites?**
I'd say it's mostly about connecting with the past. Historical sites let you almost step back in time and picture how earlier generations actually lived, which textbooks can't quite capture. Plus there's the curiosity factor — people love seeing famous places they've only ever read about. For me, that sense of awe is the real pull.

**Q5. Do you think it's reasonable to charge an entry fee for visiting interesting buildings?**
I think it's perfectly fair, to be honest. Maintaining grand old buildings costs a fortune, and entry fees help cover repairs and upkeep that taxes alone couldn't. But I'd argue the price should stay reasonable, since charging too much would shut ordinary visitors out. A modest fee feels like the right middle ground.

**Q6. Is it better to live in a new building or an old one?**
For me, a new building wins, mainly because modern places come with better insulation, lifts and decent plumbing that just work. Old buildings have bags of character, sure, but they often hide draughts and pricey maintenance headaches. Having said that, I totally get why some folk fall for the charm of an older home.
