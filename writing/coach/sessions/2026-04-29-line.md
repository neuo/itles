**题目**：The graph below shows average carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions per person in the United Kingdom, Sweden, Italy and Portugal between 1967 and 2007.

| Year | UK | Sweden | Italy | Portugal |
|------|-----|--------|-------|----------|
| 1967 | 11 | 9 | 4 | 1.5 |
| 1977 | 11 | 10.5 | 6 | 2 |
| 1987 | 10 | 8 | 7 | 4 |
| 1997 | 10 | 6.5 | 7.5 | 5 |
| 2007 | 9 | 5.5 | 8 | 5.5 |

（单位：metric tonnes per person）

## suzy 原文

The graph illustrates the change of carbon dioxide output per capita across four different countries — the United Kingdom, Sweden, Italy and Portugal — over a 40-year period from 1967, measured in metric tonnes.

Overall, the carbon emissions of the United Kingdom and Sweden followed a downward trend, whereas Italy and Portugal experienced a steady increase in their emissions. Despite the decline, the UK remained the frontrunner throughout the whole period.

In 1969, the UK had the largest emissions at roughly 11 metric tonnes per person. The figure decreased steadily to around 9 metric tonnes by 2007. Sweden followed a more volatile path, it peaked at just over 10 tonnes in 1977 before fell significantly to approximately 5.5 tonnes by the end of period.

In contrast, Italy saw its emissions rise consistently from just above 4 tonnes in 1967 to a plateau of about 8 tonnes between 1997 and 2007, surpassing Sweden in approximately 1990. Similarly, Portugal, which started with the lowest emissions at roughly 1 tonne in 1967, witnessed a fourfold growth to reach about 5.5 tonnes by 2007, eventually equaling Sweden's level

## Band 7 范文

The graph illustrates average CO₂ emissions per person in four European countries — the UK, Sweden, Italy and Portugal — between 1967 and 2007, measured in metric tonnes.

Overall, the UK and Sweden both saw their emissions decline over the period, while Italy and Portugal recorded steady increases. Despite falling, the UK remained the highest-emitting country throughout.

In 1967, the UK had the highest emissions at approximately 11 metric tonnes per person, declining gradually to around 9 tonnes by 2007. Sweden followed a more volatile trajectory, peaking at just over 10 tonnes in 1977 before falling sharply to approximately 5.5 tonnes by the end of the period.

By contrast, Italy's emissions rose consistently from just above 4 tonnes in 1967, levelling off at around 8 tonnes between 1997 and 2007 and overtaking Sweden at around 1990. Portugal, starting from the lowest point of roughly 1.5 tonnes, saw fourfold growth over the four decades, reaching approximately 5.5 tonnes by 2007 — matching Sweden's final figure.
