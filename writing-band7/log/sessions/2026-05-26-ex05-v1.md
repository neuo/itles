## 修复版（只改硬伤 + 关键升级）

### Intro
There are two questions to consider about the **rise** of solo living: why it has become so common, and whether the trend is beneficial to society as a whole. In my view, although it reflects greater personal freedom, its overall effect is negative.

### Body 1
**[T]** The main causes of living alone are changes in the economy and attitudes towards marriage.
**[E1]** **Regarding the economy**, rising incomes allow people to move out and establish their own homes. Furthermore, modern careers often require young people to relocate to major cities for work, making it impractical to live with a large extended family.
**[E2]** At the social level, attitudes towards marriage have shifted noticeably. **An increasing number of people pursue independence in lifestyle and remain unmarried**, which can reduce daily conflicts over habits.
**[Ex]** A case in point is Japan, where **delaying marriage and remaining single** have **led to cities** where nearly half of households are single-person.
**[L]** Therefore, economic independence and the pursuit of **personal independence** are driving the decline of the traditional extended family.

### Body 2
**[T]** Although solo living can bring some benefits for individuals, its broader effect on society is mostly negative.
**[E1]** The energy and land consumption of a person living alone is significantly higher than that of a bigger family, which puts extra pressure on **the environment**.
**[E2]** The popularity of single-person **households** also leads to **weaker community bonds** and exacerbates social isolation.
**[Ex]** For example, health research in the United Kingdom has found that older citizens living alone for long periods **have higher rates of** depression and **cognitive decline**.
**[L]** **These examples demonstrate that** personal gains from solo living come with environmental and social costs.

### Conclusion
The trend towards living alone is fueled by the combination of economic independence and changing attitudes towards family. While solo living **gives individuals greater personal freedom**, its effects on society, mental health, and resource use are **mostly** negative.
